UPDATE: India's Education Minister resigns, ending historic student protests over exam leaks

What happened

Indian Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned on Saturday following weeks of intense, youth-led protests over leaked national exam papers. The President immediately appointed Cabinet Minister Pralhad Joshi to replace him. Following the resignation, the Cockroach Janta Party, the youth group spearheading the protests, agreed to call off its month-long demonstrations at Jantar Mantar. The government agreed to the group's key demands, promising financial compensation for the families of students who died by suicide after the leaks and agreeing to drop all police charges against protesters.

Why it matters

This marks a historic concession by the administration of Prime Minister Narendra Modi, which rarely yields to public pressure. Previously, we reported on the massive, youth-led protests over the paper leaks, and we were watching for the government's response to the students' petition. This resignation, which comes right after activist Sonam Wangchuk ended his hunger strike, shows how the combined pressure of youth protesters and opposition lawmakers successfully forced the government's hand.

What to watch next

Watch for the newly appointed minister, Pralhad Joshi, to officially take charge of the education ministry. Look for the government to introduce its tough new anti-paper-leak bill in Parliament on Monday, July 27, 2026. Monitor the distribution of the promised compensation to the families of affected students and the formal withdrawal of police cases.

Berlin Pride cancelled and manhunt launched after vehicle strikes crowd

What happened

A vehicle drove into a crowd near Berlin's annual Christopher Street Day Pride event in Tiergarten park on Saturday night, killing at least one person and injuring 15 others. A heavily damaged white vehicle was found abandoned at the scene, and police have launched an intensive manhunt for the driver. Organisers immediately cancelled the remaining Pride activities, and authorities urged hundreds of thousands of participants to go home.

Why it matters

Although investigators have not yet confirmed if the crash was intentional, Berlin Mayor Kai Wegner described it as a brutal attack on "our free and open society." Christopher Street Day is one of Europe's largest LGBTQ+ Pride celebrations, drawing massive crowds to the German capital. Germany remains on high alert for extremist violence, and any confirmed attack on a Pride event would spark deep concerns over rising hate crimes against minority groups in Europe.

What to watch next

Watch for Berlin police to release updates on the manhunt and identify the suspect or suspects. Monitor statements from investigators regarding the driver's motive as they examine the abandoned vehicle. Look for any upcoming public vigils or security updates from Berlin city officials in the next 48 hours.

Words to know

Tiergarten TEER-gar-tun
A massive, famous public park located in the center of Berlin, Germany. It was the location where the vehicle struck the crowd.
Christopher Street Day KRIS-tuh-fur street day
The European name for annual LGBTQ+ Pride parades and festivals. The events are held to commemorate the 1969 Stonewall riots in New York.

Ebola outbreak in the DRC intensifies with deaths surpassing 1,300

What happened

A deadly Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has rapidly intensified, with the death toll surging past 1,300. Government figures show confirmed cases and deaths rose by over 40 percent in just five days, reaching 2,973 cases. To make matters worse, medical workers in the hard-hit Ituri province have gone on strike to protest unpaid wages and unsafe conditions, leaving major treatment centres paralyzed.

Why it matters

Health experts warn this is the fastest-spreading Ebola epidemic ever documented, reaching 1,000 deaths in under 10 weeks compared to eight months during the historic 2013-2016 West African outbreak. It is driven by the Bundibugyo strain, a rare variant of the virus for which there are no approved vaccines or treatments. Because the outbreak is unfolding in an active conflict zone, medical teams face extreme violence, including attacks on hospitals by local residents who are suspicious of foreign medical aid.

What to watch next

Monitor the progress of a clinical trial in the coming weeks, following the first dose of an experimental vaccine developed by the University of Oxford. Watch for whether the DRC government negotiates an end to the healthcare workers' strike in Bunia. Track upcoming announcements from the Africa CDC on emergency funding or international medical deployments.

Words to know

Bundibugyo boon-dee-BOO-joh
A rare variant of the Ebola virus named after a district in Uganda. It currently lacks any approved vaccine or treatment.
Africa CDC AF-ri-kuh see-dee-SEE
The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention. This is the African Union's public health agency that helps African nations respond to disease outbreaks.
Ituri ee-TOO-ree
A northeastern province of the Democratic Republic of Congo. It is currently the epicentre of the fast-spreading Ebola outbreak.

Syngenta to withdraw Parkinson's-linked herbicide paraquat from Australia

What happened

Swiss chemical manufacturer Syngenta announced it will stop selling its herbicide, paraquat, in Australia. The company cited new safety restrictions introduced by Australian regulators in June, which made selling the chemical "commercially unviable." These regulations phased out backpack sprayers, lowered application rates, and mandated extra protective gear, although Australian regulators maintain there is insufficient proof linking paraquat to Parkinson's disease.

Why it matters

Paraquat is already banned in more than 70 countries, including the European Union, due to its high toxicity. However, it is still widely used in Australian agriculture to manage weeds and herbicide resistance in crops like grains, sugarcane, and cotton. While Syngenta is pulling its brand-name products, local farmers will still have access to generic versions of paraquat, sparking ongoing concerns among neurologists who cite real-world studies showing the chemical triples the risk of developing Parkinson's disease.

What to watch next

Watch for when Syngenta's official phase-out timelines are finalized under Australian regulatory oversight. Monitor whether Australian farming groups pressure the government to approve alternative weed killers to prevent crop losses. Track if Australian health advocates launch new legal challenges to ban generic versions of paraquat.

Words to know

Paraquat PA-ruh-kwat
A highly toxic, fast-acting chemical herbicide used to destroy weeds and grass. It has been banned in many countries due to severe health risks.
Epidemiological eh-pi-dee-mee-uh-LO-ji-kul
Relating to the scientific study of how diseases spread, occur, and can be controlled in different human populations.

Over one million US children lose food stamps following federal welfare cuts

What happened

A new report reveals that more than one million American children have lost access to federal food benefits since July 2025. The cuts occurred after the US Congress passed the Republican-sponsored "One Big Beautiful Bill Act," which shifted food assistance costs to individual states and tightened eligibility. More than four million Americans in total have been removed from the food assistance program, known as SNAP or food stamps, with states like Arizona, Louisiana, and Texas seeing the steepest declines.

Why it matters

SNAP is the largest anti-hunger program in the US, helping low-income households buy groceries. Proponents of the welfare cuts argue they reduce government spending and encourage work, but municipal leaders and anti-poverty groups warn of a worsening food security crisis. With over 200 US mayors petitioning the Senate to reverse the cuts, the issue has sparked an intense partisan debate between Republicans defending the legislation and progressive Democrats trying to restore the funding.

What to watch next

Watch for whether the US Senate responds to the bipartisan appeal from the US Conference of Mayors to reverse the food stamp cuts. Look for any upcoming legal challenges by civil rights groups or states seeking to block the cost-shifting rules. Monitor local food bank reports in late 2026 to see how charities are coping with the increased demand.

Words to know

SNAP snap
The Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program. This is a US federal government program that provides food-purchasing assistance for low- and no-income families.
Reconciliation reh-kun-si-lee-AY-shun
A special parliamentary process in the US Congress. It allows tax and spending bills to pass with a simple majority, bypassing the usual voting delays.
